==================================================
1. PURPOSE OF THIS UPDATE
==================================================

The discovery must explicitly capture:
- The primary buyer and what they want.
- Practical, internal, and philosophical problems.
- The company's empathy and credibility.
- The customer's path to value.
- Direct and lower-commitment actions.
- Immediate operational results.
- Business, team, and personal success.
- Credible consequences of leaving the problem unresolved.
- Optional buyer transformation.
- Outcomes and concerns for relevant stakeholders.

Do not infer all of this from broad questions about business goals.

Separate:
- What success means for the founder's engagement with Rishari.
- What success means for the buyer of the client's offer.

Use plain-language questions.
Clients do not need to understand marketing framework terminology.

==================================================
2. FIX THE ALTERNATIVES QUESTIONS
==================================================

Keep company-level and offer-level alternatives separate.

Company-level:
"If customers did not work with your company, how would they
address the broader need you help with?"

Offer-level:
"If a customer did not choose [offer name], what would they use
or do instead to solve this particular problem?"

For planned offers:
"How are intended customers handling this problem today,
before [offer name] is available?"

Conditional probes:
- "What are they using or doing today?"
- "Why might they reasonably stay with that approach?"
- "What would have to change for them to consider switching?"

Use the actual offer name.
Do not refer to a product when the offer is a service.
Use "offer" internally as the neutral data-model term.

==================================================
3. INTEGRATE THE QUESTIONS, DO NOT DUPLICATE THEM
==================================================

Map these additions to the existing modules.

Reuse an existing answer if it already supplies the information.
Ask the client to confirm or clarify rather than repeat it.

Do not add a second questionnaire after the current discovery.

Use:
- Deterministic routing first.
- Limited AI clarification second.
- One main thinking task per screen.
- Optional probes only when the answer warrants them.
- "I'm not sure yet."
- "Discuss with Rishari."

Preserve the existing two-follow-up limit per core question.

Do not require every optional probe to be answered.

==================================================
4. CHARACTER: BUYER AND DESIRED OUTCOME
==================================================

Client explanation:
"A business has goals, but a person still has to make the decision.
We want to understand what that person is trying to accomplish."

Core:
- "For [offer name], who is the main person we need to persuade?"
- "What are they responsible for?"
- "What are they trying to accomplish when they start looking
   for help?"

Conditional:
- "Which outcome matters most?"
- "Who is depending on them to get this right?"
- "What makes this a priority now?"

Capture separately:
- Organization type.
- Buyer role.
- Desired outcome.
- Responsibilities.
- Trigger.

An industry or company-size description alone does not complete
the buyer-character fields.

==================================================
5. PROBLEM: PRACTICAL, INTERNAL, AND PHILOSOPHICAL
==================================================

Client explanation:
"We want to understand what is going wrong, what it is like
to deal with, and why it matters."

Core:
- "What is getting in the way of the outcome they want?"
- "What does dealing with that problem make their working day
   look like?"
- "What about this situation feels unnecessarily difficult,
   unfair, or unacceptable?"

Conditional:
- "Does it affect their confidence, stress, or sense of control?
   If so, how?"
- "Who else gets pulled into the problem?"
- "What do they have to explain or apologize for?"

Store practical, internal, and philosophical problems separately.

Do not invent emotions.
Do not force moral language onto a practical purchase.
Unknown or not applicable are valid responses.

==================================================
6. GUIDE: EMPATHY AND CREDIBILITY
==================================================

Client explanation:
"People need to feel understood and have a truthful reason
to feel confident taking the next step."

Core:
- "What do you understand about their situation that helps
   them feel understood?"
- "How does that understanding affect the way you serve them?"
- "What can you truthfully show or explain that gives them
   confidence in your ability to help?"

Accept:
Relevant experience, a prototype, a demonstration, a method,
qualifications, early feedback, or established customer evidence.

Do not require testimonials or measured results.
Do not treat founder career experience as new-company results.

Separate:
- Empathy.
- Credibility.
- Evidence.
- Current capability.
- Planned capability.

==================================================
7. PLAN: CUSTOMER PATH AND COMMITMENTS
==================================================

Client explanation:
"A clear path helps someone understand what happens next
and what will be expected of them."

Core:
- "What are the main steps from exploring [offer name]
   to receiving value?"
- "What does the customer do, and what do you handle?"
- "What commitments or explanations help reduce uncertainty?"

Conditional:
- "What happens during onboarding or implementation?"
- "What support is available?"
- "What does the buyer learn before committing?"
- "What limitations or responsibilities should they understand?"

Distinguish:
- How the product or method works.
- How the customer evaluates, adopts, and receives value.

Do not force three steps.
Do not invent guarantees.

==================================================
8. ACTION: READY NOW AND NOT READY YET
==================================================

Core:
- "What should someone do when they are ready to move forward?"
- "What happens after they take that step?"
- "What useful smaller step is available if they are interested
   but not ready to commit?"

Do not invent a free consultation, trial, downloadable guide,
or other action that the company does not offer.

"No smaller step exists yet" is valid.

==================================================
9. IMMEDIATE RESULT
==================================================

Client explanation:
"We want to distinguish the first change your offer creates
from the larger outcomes that change may enable."

Core:
- "What is the first observable change after adoption or delivery?"
- "What needs to happen for that change to occur?"

Capture dependencies where relevant.

Immediate Result is a Rishari extension to the narrative structure.
Do not label it as a separate canonical StoryBrand element.

==================================================
10. SUCCESS
==================================================

Client explanation:
"You don't need measured results to describe success.
Tell us what a good outcome would look like, and we'll separate
expected benefits from outcomes already demonstrated."

Core:
- "If this works well, what is different for the business?"
- "What would success mean for the person choosing or using it?"
- "How would they recognize that it is working?"

Conditional:
- "What becomes easier for the team?"
- "What might their manager or colleagues notice?"
- "Does anyone outside the organization benefit?"
- "Are there measures they already use to judge success?"
- "Over what realistic period might those changes appear?"

Separate:
- Immediate result.
- Organizational success.
- Team success.
- Personal success.
- Downstream benefit.
- Measures or observable signals.
- Time horizon.
- Dependencies.

Do not require numeric targets.
Do not turn expected benefits into proven claims.

==================================================
11. FAILURE AND STAKES
==================================================

Client explanation:
"We're not looking for scare tactics. We want to understand
the realistic cost of leaving the problem unresolved."

Core:
- "If nothing changes, what is likely to continue happening?"
- "What could become harder, more costly, or more risky?"
- "Who carries those consequences?"
- "What would the buyer most want to prevent?"

Conditional:
- "What have you observed, and what are you anticipating?"
- "What supports that expectation?"
- "Which consequences should we avoid overstating?"

Keep separate:
- Consequences of the unresolved problem.
- Concerns about adopting this offer.
- Risks of an unsuitable solution.

Never imply that declining this offer inevitably causes failure.
Do not invent financial, professional, clinical, or safety harm.

==================================================
12. TRANSFORMATION: OPTIONAL
==================================================

Client explanation:
"Some purchases change how people see their role.
Others simply solve a practical problem. Either is useful."

Questions:
- "If relevant, how would they want others to describe them
   or their team after success?"
- "What would they no longer want to be known for?"

Do not force an identity story.
Mark not applicable when appropriate.

13. STAKEHOLDER OUTCOMES
==================================================

Add an adaptive stakeholder map.

Possible roles:
- Primary buyer.
- Internal champion.
- User.
- Approver or evaluator.
- Downstream beneficiary.

For relevant roles, capture:
- Desired outcome.
- What they want to prevent.
- What they need to believe.
- Role in the decision.
- Evidence basis or uncertainty.

One person may occupy several roles.
Do not automatically create five personas.
Do not repeat the entire questionnaire for every stakeholder.

Keep one primary character for each individual narrative.
Create tailored variants only when needs materially differ.

14. EVIDENCE AND COVERAGE
==================================================

For narrative inputs, distinguish:
- Observed.
- Customer-reported.
- Founder belief.
- Planned.
- Unknown.

Keep evidence status separate from external-use approval.

Coverage statuses:
- Not asked.
- Captured sufficiently.
- Needs clarification.
- Hypothesis to test.
- Not applicable.

Do not use a numerical readiness score.

Missing customer proof is not an error.
Unsupported claims require attention.

15. ADMIN GUIDANCE AND FLAGS
==================================================

Add flag types for:
- Audience without a specific buyer.
- Buyer without a clear desired outcome.
- Success described only as product functionality.
- Personal or team stakes unknown.
- Failure stated as an unsupported worst-case scenario.
- Plan describing technology but not the customer's path.
- Empathy or credibility unclear.
- Current and planned outcomes blended.
- Several stakeholders blended into one buyer.

Every flag must contain:
- Source references.
- What we noticed.
- Suggested action.
- Simple example.
- Why it matters.
- Suggested client question.
- Reviewer disposition.

Example:

Issue:
Success is described only as functionality.

What we noticed:
"The answer explains automated reminders but not what improves
for the buyer."

Suggested action:
Clarify the outcome the functionality is intended to enable.

Example:
"'Automated reminders' describes a capability.
'Less time spent chasing responses' describes an intended benefit.
That benefit still needs to be confirmed, not assumed."

Why it matters:
"Buyers need to understand why the capability matters to their work."

Suggested question:
"If the reminders work as intended, what becomes easier,
and how would the customer notice?"
